# Teach

Use only when explicitly requested. Teach toward a concrete goal, not a topic inventory. Use supplied or project-local evidence only. Do not fill evidence gaps with confident recall or fetch external resources. Ask for suitable sanitized material when needed.

## Start and continue

1. Agree on the teaching workspace and read existing mission, glossary, and learning records if supplied or present there. Do not create a scaffold just because files are missing.
2. Ask why the user wants to learn and what observable success looks like. Obtain user approval of the mission before saving it; confirm all later mission changes with the user. Keep unrelated goals in separate workspaces.
3. Use demonstrated understanding and explicitly stated prior knowledge to choose a task just beyond current ability. Record claimed depth as claimed, not tested.
4. Teach one short, self-contained Markdown lesson tied to the mission. Include only knowledge needed for one tangible win. Identify supporting local evidence and its limits.
5. Give a small exercise, wait for the answer, and provide specific corrective feedback. Avoid answer clues from formatting. Invite follow-up questions and adjust difficulty from the response.
6. Separate immediate fluency from durable retention. Use recall without notes, later spaced practice, and mixed related exercises for skills practice. Do not call exposure mastery.

## Inline learning formats

Save only approved Markdown at agreed local paths. Preserve existing material and create directories only when a real entry is needed.

- **MISSION.md:** topic title; **Why** with a concrete goal; **Success looks like** with observable abilities; **Constraints** such as time and preferences; **Out of scope**. Keep it short and include only confirmed facts.
- **Lesson:** title; mission-linked outcome; brief explanation and evidence; exercise; feedback after the response; next recall prompt. Use sequential numbers and a short slug if saving multiple lessons. Keep each lesson independently understandable.
- **GLOSSARY.md:** topic title and grouped terms if useful. Each bold canonical term has a one- or two-sentence definition, aliases to avoid, and any resolved ambiguity. Add a term only after the user uses it correctly. Use canonical terms consistently and revise stale definitions as understanding improves.
- **Learning record:** sequential number and short title, then one to three sentences stating what was demonstrated or claimed and why it changes future teaching. Add evidence or implications only when useful. Scan existing numbers before appending. Mark an outdated record “superseded by” the replacement number; preserve its history.
- **Reference note:** optional compact Markdown summary of a demonstrated lesson, such as a checklist or glossary. Include needed explanations inline, not required links.
- **Preference note:** exact user-stated teaching preference, saved only with approval. Do not infer private personal facts.

Write learning records for demonstrated nontrivial understanding, stated prior knowledge with its claimed depth, corrected misconceptions, or an approved mission shift. Do not log mere coverage, routine activity, or duplicate glossary definitions. Require approval before durable record edits.

If evidence cannot support a lesson or practical judgment needs direct observation, state the gap and ask for suitable local evidence or qualified human feedback. Do not invent expertise, endorse an unknown community, or claim practice was observed. Report the lesson outcome, demonstrated versus pending understanding, approved saved files, and next practice.
